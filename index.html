Agent Orchestra

One protocol for multi-agent work: who does what, who may write, who verifies, and what counts as done.

What it settles

  • Who does what. Roles are assigned from a capability-scored roster you declare - three agents work, so do twelve, and the names are yours.
  • One writer per resource. A task can only be claimed by its owner; a second writer is refused rather than merged by accident.
  • A gate no task can skip. Completion needs a verification PASS from an agent that is not the implementer; a human override costs an approver, a scope and a reason, and the failure stays on the record.
  • Evidence with a grade. E1 means someone else can re-run it; a bare sentence is stored as E3 and labelled, so self-report cannot pose as proof.
  • Rigor that decides the work. L1 local, L2 shared, L3 consequential - L2 and L3 cannot finish without graded evidence, and L3 additionally requires E1 and a verifier that produced none of it.
  • A handoff that survives. Four shared files carry status, owner, next step and evidence, and check-handoff verifies the record before a successor trusts it.

Checks you can re-run

  • node scripts/gate.mjs - one command for CI and your machine: config truth, activation behaviour, the engine, the CLI gate, the handoff record, doc claims. It injects ten known faults and must reject every one before it reports PASS.
  • node scripts/check-handoff.mjs --dir <shared-dir> - the handoff record, checked rather than described.
  • node scripts/detect-trigger.mjs --text "<request>" - three-valued activation: 0 engage, 3 ambiguous (you decide), 1 stay silent.

Open source: the code is Apache-2.0, the protocol specification is CC BY 4.0. It cannot promise infallibility, and it never replaces your authorization for irreversible actions.
